## Product objective and execution decision

Build an **evidence-driven IAM governance platform with constrained AI assistance**. Its first customer promise is to help an IAM administrator collect traceable identity and policy relationships, help a reviewer decide on one observed grant path with its source and uncertainty visible, and help an auditor reconstruct the evidence and decision later. The product must work when AI is disabled. It does not establish effective AWS permissions or business need from a policy attachment alone.

The first buyer hypothesis is the IAM/security team that owns recurring certification and audit work. The primary users are the collection administrator, assigned reviewer, and auditor; remediation approvers and security analysts enter later workflows. Test whether the expensive step is locating the grant source, interpreting missing evidence, deciding what to escalate, or assembling an audit trail. Measure **correct, defensible reviews per unit of reviewer effort**, including path identification, uncertainty recognition, appropriate escalation, and export completeness. A faster answer that worsens any of those outcomes is a failure.

**Release 1, trustworthy governance foundation**, is the first customer milestone. It covers the bounded, reviewable experience behind `FIN-01`–`FIN-30`, plus `FIN-38` (AI-independent operation). Phase 0 supplies its evidence and risk contracts; a Release 1 experience pass must then close the remaining source-comparison, path, statement, and audit usability gaps. The first *AI product-release* claim comes later: the existing explanation task helps explain why one observed grant path was flagged, cites frozen evidence, and states what the platform cannot infer, after that claim passes evaluation.

| Delivery track | Product outcome | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 0: deterministic foundation | Trustworthy collection, provenance, risk uncertainty, and review API/UI contracts | Implement and validate first |
| Release 1 experience and acceptance | Reviewer can trace, assess, decide or escalate, and export one grant path end to end; administrator can inspect scan and history state | Complete before calling Release 1 shipped |
| Product discovery | Confirm buyer, recurring review pain, simpler alternatives, willingness to pilot, and the value of conversation | Run alongside foundation work |
| Phase 1: existing explanation evaluation | Establish whether review-scoped AI adds safe, measurable utility | Gate separately after deterministic baseline |
| Phases 2–3: review Q&A and pilot | Add and validate a narrow copilot only if discovery and Phase 1 justify it | Conditional Release 2 |
| Phases 4–8 | Policy intelligence, paths, analytics, remediation, and compliance | Separate investment and release decisions |

Phase 0 executes in dependency order: **P0.1 contracts and compatibility audit → P0.2 evidence and publication correctness → P0.3 deterministic risk assessment → P0.4 API and reviewer workspace**. Release 1 experience acceptance follows these packages. An existing guarantee should be verified and reused before adding a new state or table.

No phase treats a policy attachment as proof of effective AWS access or an AI-generated proposal as proof that a change is safe. AWS authorization depends on policy type and request context, including identity and resource policies, explicit denies, boundaries, SCPs, RCPs, and session policies. See [AWS policy evaluation logic](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/reference_policies_evaluation-logic.html). Even [AWS's policy simulator](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/access_policies_testing-policies.html) documents limits relative to live behavior.

## Product story and release map

The first complete journey is **collect → inspect coverage and source → review one pinned grant path → decide or escalate with a reason → export historical evidence and decision**. The UI should answer what was observed, why it was flagged, what is unknown, how old the evidence is, and where to inspect the exact supporting statement. A scan that fails or is superseded is visible as such. A historical review remains readable after its source becomes stale or invalidated; current eligibility is shown separately.

## Repository baseline and existing guarantees

- `common/evidence.py` records stable grant and observation identities and policy hashes; `common/source_scanner.py` and `lambdas/identity_discovery/handler.py` collect scope-specific observations.
- `common/repo.py` stores immutable published snapshots and assessments. A source attempt advances its own attempt head; successful publication atomically stores that source's observations and advances its publication pointer. A later failed or superseded attempt does **not** erase the prior publication, but currently blocks new campaign and live-remediation eligibility until a new eligible publication. Different source scopes are **not** one atomic cross-source snapshot.
- `lambdas/risk_evaluation/handler.py` performs policy-document triage. New assessments use `POLICY_RULE_VERSION=policy-risk-v6` with separate severity, confidence, completeness, and grant evidence. Legacy `risk_level=HIGH` still means either a concerning pattern **or** safe routing for unresolved evidence.
- `common/ai_artifacts.py` and `lambdas/ai_explanation/handler.py` implement only `REVIEW_EXPLANATION` for eligible pending HIGH reviews. Provider input is minimized; schema and review-local citation IDs are checked, but textual entailment is not. Jobs have leases, bounded retries and a deterministic fallback. `ai_jobs` and `ai_artifacts` constrain `task_type` to `REVIEW_EXPLANATION`.
- `api/review_service.py` enforces review and account access for current routes; `frontend/src/app/api/backend/[...path]/route.ts` has an explicit route allowlist. `frontend/src/components/review-workspace.tsx` warns that equivalent access has not been evaluated.
- `docs/production-readiness.md` lists absent deployment controls. The repository has no real provider evaluation, managed AWS staging, worker fleet, or provider/privacy acceptance proven by local tests.

Keep these distinctions in every design review: **stored history**, **current source eligibility**, **authorization analysis**, and **business impact** are separate questions.

## Architecture contracts to settle in Phase 0

### A. Policy-type and claim contract

Use [the Phase 0 IAM semantics contract](iam-semantics-contract.md). Define a typed representation before extending classification:

`PolicyDocument = {policy_type, version, parse_status, statements, unsupported_constructs, source_evidence_id, document_hash}`. A statement records its zero-based index and optional `Sid`, `Effect`, `Action` or `NotAction`, `Resource` or `NotResource`, `Principal` or `NotPrincipal` where applicable, and a structured `Condition` presence/status. Preserve the original frozen document. Parsing a construct is **not** interpreting its authorization effect.

| Policy or relationship | Phase 0 supported claim | Phase 0 boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IAM user/group/role identity policy | An attachment and specified statements were observed; a rule matched syntax or an exact reviewed action | No principal effective-permission claim |
| IAM role trust policy | A trust statement names principals or contains broad/conditional trust | No proof the named caller can assume the role |
| Identity Center account assignment | An assignment path and available permission-set documents were observed | No expansion of Identity Center group members or complete user access |
| Resource policy, permissions boundary, SCP, RCP, session policy | Mark as uncollected or unevaluated unless an independently scoped source is added later | No inferred allow/deny conclusion |
| `NotAction`, `NotResource`, `NotPrincipal`, conditions, explicit Deny | Preserve exact construct and classify evaluation as partial/unsupported where semantics are not implemented | Do not interpret an exclusion as narrow access or subtract Deny from Allow using string matching |

Each public claim has a type: `OBSERVED_FACT`, `RULE_INFERENCE`, `EXTERNAL_FINDING`, or `NOT_EVALUATED`. Only the first two are currently generated by this platform. For each claim, record its exact evidence reference, rule version, scope, and permitted wording. Unsupported constructs generate a manual-review reason, never a silent LOW classification. Parser acceptance covers duplicate JSON keys, invalid JSON/versions, malformed statements and types, mixed effects/conditions, changed managed-policy versions, and missing attachment metadata. Bind grant-level assessments to the exact observation, attachment, policy version and hash; policy hash alone does not establish attachment identity. Build an independently reviewed scenario corpus rather than tests that merely repeat the current classifier's output.

Rule aggregation is deterministic but **does not perform IAM authorization evaluation**. For each document: (1) identify policy type; (2) parse every statement and preserve its exact source; (3) record unsupported constructs; (4) classify each statement independently against reviewed triage rules; (5) collect all concerning patterns without treating Deny as an automatic cancellation of Allow; (6) derive assessment status from what was interpreted; (7) apply the confidence rubric; and (8) set manual review independently of severity. A policy can therefore be `risk_severity=HIGH`, `assessment_status=PARTIAL`, `assessment_confidence=MEDIUM`, and `manual_review_required=true` at once. The reason list retains both a wildcard pattern and an unsupported `NotAction`. Rule precedence applies only to **triage ordering** after all reasons are retained; it never claims a final allow/deny result. AWS documents that `NotAction` with Allow can match many applicable actions, depending on resource scope: [NotAction semantics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/reference_policies_elements_notaction.html).

### B. Risk, confidence, and completeness contract

Use [the Phase 0 severity and completeness rubric](risk-assessment-model.md). Keep the existing `risk_level` HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW as a **legacy workflow triage field** until campaign, API, AI eligibility, and report consumers are migrated. Add a versioned assessment payload for new rule versions:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `risk_severity`: CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/UNKNOWN | Severity of an identified concerning pattern, **not** effective-access severity |
| `assessment_status`: SUPPORTED/PARTIAL/UNASSESSED/UNSUPPORTED | How far the document was interpreted under this rule version |
| `assessment_confidence`: HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/INSUFFICIENT | Confidence in the **rule-based document conclusion**, not a probability of AWS authorization; use only with a written rubric |
| `evidence_completeness` | Separate collection status, source coverage, document coverage, interpretability, missing-reference count, unsupported constructs, and reason codes |
| `manual_review_required` and `reason_codes[]` | Why human investigation is needed; may coexist with a known high-severity pattern |
| `statement_refs[]` | Matched statement index/Sid, action or construct, and frozen document hash |

Example: wildcard administrative syntax may be `risk_severity=HIGH` with a supported pattern; a missing document is `risk_severity=UNKNOWN`, `assessment_confidence=INSUFFICIENT`, and `manual_review_required=true`. Both may remain legacy `risk_level=HIGH` for safe routing, but the UI and AI must display their different meaning. A complete scan can still contain an uninterpretable policy. Old pinned assessments without the new payload remain labeled legacy/unknown; do not backfill confidence by guessing from the old HIGH value.

`assessment_confidence` means confidence that the stated **document triage** follows from the available document and implemented rules. It is neither a calibrated probability nor confidence that AWS will authorize a request. `SUPPORTED` does not imply complete source coverage. The rubric must define which constructs permit HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, or INSUFFICIENT confidence and be reviewed against independently labeled cases before this field is exposed.

Implement the new payload in the existing immutable `policy_risk_assessments.evidence` JSON with an explicit schema version if this meets query needs. If Phase 0 requires indexed queries by the new fields, add **new nullable columns** in the next numbered migration instead; keep old rows intact and test SQLite/PostgreSQL parity. Bump `POLICY_RULE_VERSION` and append new assessments. Do not modify an applied migration or rewrite an old review binding.

### C. Snapshot and review-evidence lifecycle

Use [the Phase 0 evidence lifecycle contract](evidence-lifecycle.md). Document the current source-scoped protocol rather than inventing a cross-source transaction:

1. Register a `RUNNING` attempt for account and source scope. The source attempt head identifies the newest attempt; it is not itself a published snapshot.
2. Collect all required pages/documents for that declared scope. Pagination, API/throttle, credential, processing, or account-verification failure records `FAILED` and publishes nothing. Missing **referenced** policy evidence that the scanner intentionally retains is a different, explicit observation state.
3. Validate identities, duplicate observations, document hashes, required fields, counts, and coverage. A successfully collected attempt can still lose publication eligibility to a newer attempt.
4. In the publication transaction, compare the attempt ID against the **current** head, write the source snapshot and observations, advance its publication pointer, and mark it `COMPLETE` only if still current. `common/repo.py` already checks this inside the transaction; P0.2 must verify it under SQLite and PostgreSQL concurrency. A losing attempt currently becomes `INCOMPLETE` with a `superseded` reason.
5. Preserve the prior publication when a later attempt fails; show it as historical evidence, but keep the current rule that a newer nonpublished attempt blocks **new** campaigns and live remediation. Existing pinned reviews remain readable with a stale/blocked indicator. A rejected or superseded attempt is never republished; recollection creates a new attempt.

Expose **two logical axes**: collection = `RUNNING/SUCCEEDED/FAILED/UNKNOWN_LEGACY`; publication = `NOT_PUBLISHED/CURRENT_PUBLISHED/PREVIOUSLY_PUBLISHED/SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_PUBLICATION/REJECTED/UNKNOWN_LEGACY`. A complete collection that loses the head is `SUCCEEDED + SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_PUBLICATION`. At the initial audit, the `INCOMPLETE`/`superseded` row could not prove collection success. P0.2 now persists a validated completion receipt before publication for new attempts in this worktree; PostgreSQL concurrency and crash/retry acceptance remain open. Old ambiguous rows remain `UNKNOWN_LEGACY`. A successful zero-finding scan requires complete pagination and validated scope/coverage/counts, never a zero grant count alone.

Use one manifest hierarchy:

1. **Published source snapshot:** immutable observations from one account and source scope.
2. **`EvidenceSetManifest`:** a versioned ordered set of one or more published snapshots, with account, scope, snapshot ID, publication and observation window, coverage, missing scopes, and temporal compatibility. Phase 0 emits `SINGLE_SOURCE` manifests only. Future multi-source sets are `MULTI_SOURCE_NON_ATOMIC`; `PARTIAL`, `STALE`, and `INELIGIBLE` are independent flags/reasons rather than mutually exclusive replacements for that structural status. A temporal-skew threshold must be defined before cross-source claims are enabled; incompatible observation windows cannot imply a contemporaneous path.
3. **`ReviewEvidenceManifest`:** immutable review-specific binding to exactly one evidence-set manifest version/hash plus pinned grant/observation/assessment IDs, attachment identity, policy version/hash, rule version, and missing-evidence inventory. A review never silently adopts a newer source publication.
4. **AI retrieval context:** the authorized, bounded subset chosen for one request, with distinct `retrieved`, `considered`, `cited`, and `unavailable` IDs.
5. **Validated claims and artifact:** structured facts checked against the review manifest; generated output references their IDs and the input manifest hash.

The IAM user scanner currently collects user policy paths, IAM group membership, and group policy attachments in **one declared user-policy scope**; group-derived paths therefore do not require a cross-source join today. The same scan is not an instantaneous AWS transaction: preserve the observation window. Role trust, Identity Center, and future cross-account/resource relationships may need an explicit multi-source set. Phase 0 establishes the manifest abstraction but does not infer new cross-source access.

Canonicalize manifests using a named schema version, `common/evidence.py`'s sorted-key compact JSON convention, UTF-8 bytes, and SHA-256. Sort set-like constituent snapshots by account/scope/snapshot ID and evidence references by stable ID; preserve meaningful sequence such as statement order. Specify missing versus null, Unicode, timestamp, and numeric rules before hashing; omit volatile current-eligibility annotations. A discovered evidence defect does not erase or mutate a manifest. Add a reasoned invalidation/supersession annotation, produce a new manifest/review as needed, and show the old artifact as historical. Define retention separately from validity.

Use consistent UTC timestamp meanings: `observed_at` is when a specific AWS item was read; `collected_at` is when source collection completed; `published_at` is when its snapshot became visible; `assessed_at` is rule execution; `review_created_at` is campaign review creation; `decision_recorded_at` is the decision event; and `superseded_at`, if recorded, is when a newer publication replaced the current pointer. Existing fields may supply some timestamps; add only missing ones with a clear owner. A Monday observation must never be described as Tuesday's live state merely because a reviewer opened it Tuesday.

## Phase 1 — Evaluate the existing explanation task

**Scope:** determine whether `REVIEW_EXPLANATION` improves reviewer correctness and efficiency while remaining grounded, private, and operationally reliable. **Excluded:** Q&A, full-policy provider input, production enablement without provider and deployment acceptance.

### Evaluation design and files

Create `tests/fixtures/ai_eval/` and `scripts/evaluate_ai_explanations.py` with **four separate datasets and reports**:

| Dataset | Purpose and minimum before a limited pilot | Interpretation |

**Idempotency and replay:** the existing explanation job key uses task, review and pinned IDs, prompt/schema/model, and generation; `ai_jobs.idempotency_key` and `ai_artifacts.job_id` are unique. Before adding the manifest or new tasks, define the key over the immutable input manifest hash and task configuration and reject a collision whose stored `input_sha256` differs. A retry must either resume the same leased attempt or create an explicitly linked generation, never publish competing artifacts for one idempotency key. Replaying identical inputs can produce different model text; preserve input reproducibility and accepted-output provenance without promising byte-identical regeneration.

**Retention:** distinguish user-visible content removal from immutable job/action/audit events. Define retention duration, legal/contractual deletion requirements, who can request removal, what hashes and references may remain, and whether a conversation becomes a tombstone. **Provider input classes:** classify fields before any prompt change. AWS action names may be low sensitivity; sanitized policy structure requires explicit approval; principal identifiers and sensitive resource names/tags are excluded by default; account-specific posture and relationship context require a separately approved purpose; credentials, tokens, and secrets are never provider input. The approval record binds provider, model, region, exact input classes, retention/logging/data-use terms, approval version, and expiry or revocation conditions. Immediately before each submission, classify the **actual final payload** and require every category to be covered by the current approval. Reject an expanded payload or changed provider/model/region/terms rather than inheriting older approval. Test a prompt change that adds a principal identifier to an otherwise approved sanitized policy payload.

Adversarial tests cover instructions in policy text, names or tags; review/account ID substitution and enumeration; reviewer reassignment; evidence substitution; duplicate jobs; malformed and oversized responses; log/error leakage; fabricated references; and attempts to route Q&A into mutations. ## Phase 2 — Narrow review Q&A

**Entry:** Phases 0 and 1 pass; discovery shows Q&A solves a repeated problem. Supported intents: explain the pinned rule, attachment, scanner observation, unevaluated construct, or next evidence to inspect. Effective access, equivalent paths after revocation, replacement-policy safety, cross-account reachability, and operational impact require abstention.

Create `docs/ai-claim-validation.md`. Define a bounded claim catalog with type, subject, value, evidence ID, manifest hash, permitted wording, and validation rule. Initial claim types include `POLICY_ATTACHMENT_OBSERVED`, `GROUP_MEMBERSHIP_OBSERVED`, `TRUST_STATEMENT_OBSERVED`, `WILDCARD_PATTERN_DETECTED`, `CONDITION_PRESENT`, `POLICY_DOCUMENT_MISSING`, `SOURCE_SCAN_INCOMPLETE`, and `AUTHORIZATION_NOT_EVALUATED`. Each type lists required evidence, permitted values and language, prohibited interpretation, and account/review/snapshot scope. A group path claim validates user, group, attachment, policy version, and observation time against the pinned manifest; it cannot become an administrator-access conclusion.

The runtime answer pipeline has **three** stages: (1) produce bounded structured claim objects for an allowed question intent; (2) deterministically check type, subject, value, evidence, scope, manifest hash, and approved wording; (3) render only validated content through controlled templates for the first release. If validation fails, return an explicit abstention plus supported facts. Any later free-form paraphrase requires a separate design and evaluation because a general post-render prose checker cannot prove the absence of new claims. Do not use an LLM-as-judge as the runtime authorization or grounding boundary. Human review and optional model-assisted scoring evaluate usefulness and residual prose errors offline.

Use the Phase 0 `ReviewEvidenceManifest`, which pins a `SINGLE_SOURCE` evidence-set manifest for today's reviews, for retrieval. For each turn, pin the same review/snapshot/rule assessment and log the retrieved, considered, cited, and unavailable evidence IDs. Current-state annotations such as a newer failed scan are disclosed separately and cannot silently replace pinned facts. Authenticate and authorize every write and read after assignment/campaign changes; define whether decided or stale reviews permit new questions. Keep the first API stateless (`POST /reviews/{review_id}/ai/questions`) unless research justifies durable history.
